01 / Where this sits

Right now, marketing is directed by the person with the least time to direct it

You. The owner, or the GM, or whoever ended up holding it.

That works, and it usually works for years. The problem is that it quietly becomes the thing deciding how fast the business can grow, because nothing moves until it has been through you first. The ads agency waits on a brief. The designer waits on feedback. The developer waits on a decision nobody else is allowed to make.

Every one of those is a small delay. Together they are the growth rate.

This page explains what it costs to hand that over, what you get, and how to start. There is no call to book. Read it, and if it fits, apply.

02 / Who this is for

This is a fit if most of these are true

Marketing already costs you money

You are spending across two or more channels — ads, SEO, content, email, design — either through suppliers or staff. This replaces that spend rather than adding to it.

Nobody internally owns the strategy

There are people doing marketing tasks. There is no one whose job is the marketing plan, the budget and the result.

You are willing to hand over direction, not just tasks

We own the budget allocation and direct the work. You approve the total and the plan. If you want to keep making every call, a supplier is a better buy than we are.

You want one team, not seven vendors

One number, one plan, one point of accountability — instead of seven retainers that do not talk to each other.

Not a fit if

You want one channel run as cheaply as possible — hire a specialist, not a department. You are pre-revenue and still finding the offer — we would be expensive guesswork. Or you want to keep directing every decision yourself, in which case you are buying execution, and this is not that.

03 / What changes

Four things that are different from the day you say yes

One team, one point of accountability

When something stops working, it is our job to have noticed before you did, and to have already started fixing it. You should not be the first person to spot a problem in your own marketing.

You stop being the routing layer

We brief your staff, direct your suppliers and own the marketing calendar. Decisions that currently wait for you to make an introduction or forward an email stop waiting.

Every channel covered, no gaps

Paid ads, SEO, content, email, social, design, video and website — run by specialists in each, working to one plan instead of seven separate conversations.

A weekly strategy meeting with a senior marketer

A standing weekly meeting with an agenda and logged actions, on site or on a call, whichever suits how you work. Not a monthly report you have to chase, and not a quarterly catch-up where things have already drifted.

04 / How it runs

Three phases, and you know what each one is for

Nothing here is a revenue promise. It is what we do and when, so you can hold us to it.

1
Weeks 1 to 4

Take it over

Audit every channel, every supplier and every account. Consolidate access. Write the marketing plan and the budget. By the end of week four the calendar is ours and the briefing stops going through you.

2
Weeks 5 to 12

Fix and build

Close the gaps the audit found — usually tracking, usually SEO, usually the site. Get every channel live against the one plan. Monthly dashboard starts, weekly strategy meeting runs.

3
Month 4 onward

Compound

Quarterly strategic review at board level, annual marketing plan and budget, and a team that is now several quarters deep in your business rather than starting over.

08 / The risk

Ninety days, and either of us can walk

You are handing over the marketing function of your business to people you have mostly read about on a page. That deserves a way out.

The 90-day exit

For the first ninety days, either side can end the engagement with fourteen days notice, for any reason, with no exit fee and no notice period beyond it. You pay for the months you have had and nothing else.

You keep everything: accounts, assets, ad account access, the marketing plan, the dashboards, the creative. All of it is built in your accounts under your ownership from day one, so there is nothing to hand back because it was never ours to hold.

After ninety days it runs month to month on thirty days notice. There is no lock-in contract and there never has been. We are not interested in keeping a client who wants to leave.

What we will not do is guarantee a revenue number. Anyone who does is either guessing or pricing the refund into the fee. We will guarantee the work, the cadence and the exit.

09 / Availability

Two per quarter, and never two in the same category

This is a real constraint, not a sales device.

A fractional CMO engagement takes roughly two full-time equivalents across our team — strategy, paid, SEO, content, design, video, web and operations — plus a senior marketer in a weekly strategy meeting. We can start two of these a quarter without the existing ones getting worse, so we start two.

Category exclusivity is part of it. We will not run this engagement for two businesses competing in the same category and market. If we are already working with someone in your space, we will tell you on day one rather than take the fee.

What that means for you

If the two spots for the quarter are taken, the honest answer is that you wait for the next one — and we will say so rather than start you badly. And if a competitor of yours applies first, the category closes.
