The goal is simple. You stop being the person marketing waits on.

  • One team accountable for every channel
  • A plan and a budget you approve once, then never have to chase
  • A number you can hold me to, reviewed every quarter

Right now, marketing in your business is directed by the person with the least time to direct it. You. The owner, or the GM, or whoever ended up holding it.

That works, and it usually works for years. The problem is that it quietly becomes the thing deciding how fast the business can grow, because nothing moves until it's been through you first. The ads agency waits on a brief. The designer waits on feedback. The developer waits on a decision nobody else is allowed to make.

Every one of those is a small delay. Together, they're your growth rate.

Between us, Michael and I have worked with hundreds of businesses and managed millions in ad spend. The team behind us covers paid ads, SEO, content, email, design, video, web development and marketing operations, all in house, all on the same team, all already working together every day.

The part that matters most: you get the founders, not a junior account manager. I'm in the weekly meeting. Michael runs the strategy. That isn't a sales promise, it's how we built the agency, and it's the reason we can only take two of these a quarter.

I'm not going to put a wall of logos here. If you want to talk to businesses we already do this for, ask and I'll put you in touch with them directly.

This won't work for you if you...

  • Are pre-revenue and still working out the offer. We'd be expensive guesswork.
  • Want one channel run as cheaply as possible. Hire a specialist, not a department.
  • Want to keep making every call yourself. Then you're buying execution, and this isn't that.
  • Want someone to blame when it doesn't work, rather than someone to fix it.

But if you...

  • Are already spending across two or more channels, through suppliers or staff
  • Have people doing marketing tasks, but nobody whose job is the plan, the budget and the result
  • Are willing to hand over direction, not just tasks
  • Want one team and one number, instead of seven retainers that don't talk to each other
  • Know that how fast you grow is currently capped by how fast you personally get through things

...then this is worth another ten minutes of your time.

The plan is simple

Phase 1: Weeks 1 to 4, take it over

We audit every channel, every account and every supplier you have, and consolidate the access into one place. Most businesses find out here that their tracking has been wrong for a while, and that nobody can say what a lead actually costs. Then we write the marketing plan and the budget, and you approve the total.

That's Phase 1. By the end of week four, the calendar is ours and the briefing stops going through you.

Phase 2: Weeks 5 to 12, fix and build

We close the gaps the audit found. It's usually tracking, usually SEO, usually the website. Then every channel goes live against the one plan: paid ads, SEO, content, email, social, design, video and web, run by the specialist in each rather than by whoever had capacity.

The monthly dashboard starts here, and so does the weekly meeting with me. Standing agenda, logged actions, on site or on a call, whichever suits how you work.

So Phase 2 gets every channel live against one plan, with a baseline to measure it against.

Phase 3: Month 4 onward, compound

Quarterly strategic review at board level. Annual marketing plan and budget. Budget reallocated between channels as the numbers tell us to, without a new quote every time.

And by then you have a team several quarters deep in your business rather than one starting over, which, as you'll see in a moment, is the thing the alternatives can't give you.

There's a way out, and it works both ways

You'd be handing the marketing function of your business to people you've mostly read about on a page. That deserves an exit.

For the first ninety days, either of us can end it with fourteen days notice, for any reason, with no exit fee. You pay for the months you've had and nothing else.

You keep everything. Accounts, assets, ad account access, the marketing plan, the dashboards, the creative. All of it is built in your accounts under your ownership from day one, so there's nothing to hand back because it was never mine to hold.

After ninety days it runs month to month on thirty days notice. There's no lock-in contract and there never has been. I'm not interested in keeping a client who wants to leave.

What I won't do is guarantee you a revenue number. Anyone who does is either guessing or has priced the refund into the fee. I'll guarantee the work, the cadence and the exit.

So there's not much risk in starting, is there?

Two a quarter, and never two in the same category

This is a real constraint, not a sales device.

One of these engagements takes roughly two full-time equivalents across the team: strategy, paid, SEO, content, design, video, web and operations, plus me in a weekly meeting. We can start two a quarter without the existing ones getting worse. So we start two.

Category exclusivity is part of it. I won't run this for two businesses competing in the same category and market. If we're already working with someone in your space, I'll tell you on day one rather than take the fee.

If the two spots for the quarter are gone, the honest answer is that you wait for the next one, and I'll say so rather than start you badly. And if a competitor of yours applies first, the category closes.
