What an $85 feed test tells you that your eyes can't

You can pick clean, well-cured hay by eye and by nose - most producers can. What nobody can see from the stack is protein, energy and fibre. That's what a lab NIR feed analysis is for, and it costs less than a single bale of good lucerne.

A guide from the team at FodderLink - NIR feed testing, Australia-wide

The eye test has a blind spot

Here's a question FodderLink's testing team fields so often it's in their FAQ: "Why do my results seem low despite good visual quality?" Because colour, smell and feel tell you how hay was cured and stored - they don't tell you what it will do in a ration.

Feed that presents beautifully can under-deliver on protein and energy. Ordinary-looking feed can test remarkably well. The difference matters every single day your stock are on it: over-supplementing burns money, under-feeding burns condition and production.

The numbers aren't a substitute for your judgment - they're the part of the picture your judgment can't reach.

What a feed test actually reports

A NIR (near-infrared) feed analysis reports up to 30 nutritional values from a properly collected sample - crude protein, metabolisable energy, sugars, fibre fractions, moisture and more. Enough for you or your nutritionist to slot the feed straight into a ration instead of guessing at one.

Where the numbers pay for themselves

Buying: a feed analysis turns negotiation into arithmetic - you're comparing cost per unit of energy and protein, not photos. Selling: a grower with test results in hand can defend a price. Feeding your own crop: you find out whether this season's cut needs supplementing before the herd tells you the hard way.
