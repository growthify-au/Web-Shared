What this measures, and what it does not

Read first

AI visibility is a new and easily overstated measure. This section is deliberately blunt about the boundaries of the data so nothing here is read as more than it is.

Scope

Every figure in this report comes from Google AI Overviews in Australia: the AI-written answer that appears above the classic results. The dataset holds 9,195,514 Australian AI responses.

It does not cover ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini as an app, Perplexity or Copilot. Those platforms do not publish their data, and the Australian dataset for ChatGPT specifically does not exist: that provider's ChatGPT coverage is United States only. So this is one window into AI search, not the whole of it.

Two further points worth knowing before reading the numbers.

  • "AI search volume" is an estimate, not measured traffic. It is the data provider's modelled figure for how much search demand sits behind the questions where a brand appears. There is no click or conversion data behind it. Treat it as a comparative scale between brands, not as visits.
  • The free tool being marketed for this does not cover Castella. The publicly available AI visibility tracker indexes software and technology platforms across 1,616 categories: SEO tools, CRM, ERP, marketing automation. There is no category for hardware, homewares, furniture or building products, and Castella is not in its index. This report is built on the underlying data API instead, which does cover any brand.

Cited is not the same as recommended

Quality of mention

There are two very different ways a brand appears in an AI answer, and only one of them puts the name in front of the reader.

  • Named in the prose. The AI writes the brand name into its answer, so the reader sees it whether or not they click anything.
  • Cited in a footnote. The brand is only a numbered reference or a bare link. The reader sees a superscript, not a name.

Read: a little over half of Castella's high-demand AI appearances put the brand name in front of the reader. The other half are effectively invisible unless someone inspects the citations. Improving that ratio is a distinct objective from appearing more often, and the pages being cited in prose skew towards the explanatory articles rather than the collection pages.

The door gap does not carry over cleanly

Correction

The SEO analysis identifies the door category as Castella's largest structural gap, roughly 50,000 monthly searches ceded entirely. We expected to find the same gap reproduced in AI answers. The data says otherwise, and the reason matters.

Why we are not reporting a door AI gap

Australian AI answers about "door handles" are dominated by car door handles. Pulling the most-cited domains for that query family returns carsales.com.au, drive.com.au, carsguide.com.au, carexpert.com.au and automotive owner forums near the top, alongside a long tail of unrelated sites. Narrowing to "interior door handles" does not fix it, because that is also an automotive part term: carsales still ranks second.

Across those door query sets, Manovella appears at rank 15 and Castella does not appear in the top 20 at all. But the set is too contaminated by a different industry to report as a hardware finding, so we are not presenting it as one.

The honest conclusion is narrower than the one we set out to test. In classic search the door gap is large, real and measurable, and it is quantified in the companion SEO report. In AI answers the door hardware question space is heavily diluted by the automotive market, which means the AI upside of entering the door category is smaller and noisier than the classic search upside. If the door range decision gets made, the case for it rests on the SEO numbers, not on these.

Read: this is worth knowing before anyone assumes AI visibility scales with search volume. It does not, at least not evenly. A category can be worth 50,000 monthly searches in classic search and still be a poor AI target because the AI answer space for those words belongs to another industry. The cabinet and finish vocabulary, where Castella is already strong, is far cleaner territory.

What to do about it

Actions

Every recommendation below points at work already specified in the April analysis. Nothing here opens a new programme, and the AI data makes the existing case stronger.

1. Publish the rest of the buyer guides

Highest value

Two design journal articles already exist and they are Castella's best-performing assets in AI answers, with the warm metal finishes piece cited more often than any collection page on the site. April specified six such guides. Two are live and winning; four are not written.

The four outstanding pieces are: how to choose cabinet handles for your kitchen, interior door handle styles explained, what to look for in lockable door handles, and drawer pulls versus drawer handles. Given the finish and style vocabulary that dominates Castella's AI presence, a piece on choosing between brushed nickel, polished nickel and chrome would likely outperform any of them and is not on the original list.

2. Defend the finish pages, now with two reasons

Urgent

The finish and style cluster is where Castella's entire AI presence sits. The companion SEO report shows the same finish pages losing classic ranking positions, with both competitors newly entering all three. The AI data raises the stakes: these are not just high-intent commercial pages, they are the only part of the site the AI reliably associates with Castella.

Losing finish rankings would cost twice, in classic search and in AI presence at the same time.

3. Improve the quality of mention, not just the count

Distinct objective

Roughly half of Castella's high-demand AI appearances are footnote citations the reader never sees as a name. The answers that do name Castella tend to be answering explanatory questions, and the pages behind them tend to be the articles. That points the same way as play 1: content that explains earns the named mention, and product pages earn the footnote.

4. Treat the retailer and editorial layer as a channel decision

Commercial, not SEO

Eleven domains are cited more often than Castella in cabinet handle answers, and most are retailers, community forums or design publishers. Being stocked by, or written about on, Bunnings, Houzz, Homes to Love or the multi-brand retailers is a different lever from ranking a page, and it is the lever that reaches the top of these answers.

This is a commercial and PR question rather than an SEO task, and it needs a decision from the business rather than a recommendation from us. We have not costed it.

5. Re-measure in a quarter

Cadence

The underlying dataset refreshes weekly and this is the first measurement, so there is no trend yet, only a baseline. A re-pull in three months would show whether the guides moved the AI mention count and whether the finish cluster held. The pull is scripted and cached, so repeating it costs under a dollar.

Method and limitations

Method
  • Source. DataForSEO LLM Mentions API, ai_optimization/llm_mentions/* endpoints, pulled 13/08/2026. Location 2036 Australia, language English, platform Google AI Overviews. The Australian dataset holds 9,195,514 AI responses.
  • Platform coverage. Google AI Overviews only. No ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini app, Perplexity or Copilot. The provider's ChatGPT dataset is United States and English only, so there is no Australian equivalent to report.
  • AI search volume is the provider's own estimated metric for demand behind the questions where a brand appears. It is not measured traffic, and no click or conversion data sits behind it. We have deliberately attached no traffic or revenue projection to it.
  • Most-cited domain rankings are rank order only. The endpoint does not expose citation counts for that view, so no quantities are stated or implied.
  • The prose against footnote split is computed across the 30 highest-demand answers of the 98, not all 98. It is produced by a tested classifier that separates brand names appearing in reader-visible text from those appearing only inside link targets and numbered references, and its output was checked by hand against the source answers.
  • Door query sets are contaminated by the automotive market and are reported as a limitation rather than a finding, as set out above.
  • Reproducibility. Every API response behind this report is cached as JSON, and every figure is mapped to its source file and field in a provenance manifest. Any number here can be traced back and re-checked without re-querying.
  • This is a first measurement. The dataset updates weekly and there is no prior pull, so nothing here is a trend. Treat the figures as a baseline.
