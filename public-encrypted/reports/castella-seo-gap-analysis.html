Read: Castella and Lo&Co are now frequently ranking side by side at the top of the same queries. On several of the head terms Castella won, Lo&Co is immediately behind it, and on the drawer terms the order is reversed. This is a closer contest than April suggested.

Read: both sources independently place Castella first or second on both keywords, through the same URLs. The larger variance on Manovella is within the normal range for two providers sampling a volatile SERP at different moments, and it does not change any conclusion in this report.

Two further checks were run directly against the Castella site rather than through any search data provider. Every URL cited in this report was confirmed to return HTTP 200, and the canonical tags were read from the served HTML. That is how the unshipped canonical consolidation noted earlier was established, and it is a stronger form of evidence than a ranking position because it reads the site's own configuration.

A check of Google through an automated browser session was attempted and abandoned: Google served a CAPTCHA, and working around that was not appropriate. A check against a Bing-backed search engine was run and then discarded as evidence, because its index differs from Google's so substantially that it says nothing either way about Google positions.

Recommended next steps

Actions

Ordered by expected impact. The first item is a reversal of April's advice and is the most urgent thing in this report.

  1. Defend the finish pages. April said maintain them; the data now says defend them. Gold, brass and black have all lost ground and both competitors have entered all three. Refresh product depth on each page, add internal links using the finish name as anchor text, confirm Product and BreadcrumbList schema is valid, and re-check positions fortnightly rather than monthly. These are the highest-intent, likely highest-margin queries in the set.
  2. Diagnose the drawer vocabulary losses. "Cabinet drawer handles" at minus 13 and "drawer pull handle" dropping out of the window entirely are the clearest regressions on the site. Check whether consolidating onto the hero page stripped the specific drawer vocabulary those terms need. A dedicated drawer pulls page, which April deprioritised, may now be the better call.
  3. Finish the canonical work. The head terms are ranking correctly without it, but the duplicate page is still live and still self-canonicalising. Completing the 301 or filtered-view retirement removes the risk of the split reasserting itself.
  4. Make the door range decision. Four months on, the question that gates this remains unanswered while both competitors have strengthened. Roughly 50,000 monthly searches are still being ceded in full, and Manovella now holds #1 on the largest term in the set.
  5. Take the magnetic door stop cluster. All three variants are now uncontested by any of the three brands, which is a cleaner opening than April found. Roughly 8,700 combined monthly searches.
  6. Connect GA4 and Search Console. Still outstanding, and still the reason none of the above can be reported in revenue rather than position.

Method and limitations

Method
  • All August positions are live SERP results (Australia, English, desktop) pulled 13/08/2026 to a depth of 30 organic results per keyword. April figures are reproduced unchanged from the 23/04/2026 analysis.
  • Position means position among organic results within the 1 to 30 window. A dash means the domain did not appear anywhere in that window.
  • Search volumes are carried forward from the April pull and were not re-measured. Treat them as directional; they may have moved.
  • Three keywords initially failed with an empty-payload error and were re-pulled successfully. Those failed calls were not charged.
  • This category is volatile, which is the reason this refresh exists. Single-point positions are directional rather than absolute, and fortnightly tracking is recommended while the finish pages are contested.
  • Desktop only. Mobile results often differ, and a mobile check is worth running before acting on the finish-page findings.
  • Page titles and H1s on this site are rendered by JavaScript, so on-page copy could not be confirmed by a server-side fetch. The canonical findings come from the served HTML and are reliable.
