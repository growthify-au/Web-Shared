The five findings that matter

Executive summary

/collections/cabinet-handles is doing the heavy lifting for Castella, holding three page-1 positions on the wider cabinet vocabulary plus five page-2 footholds on drawer and pull variants. What follows is where that strength is being diluted, and where it is absent entirely.

01

The cabinet handle cluster is a three-way contest, not a Castella deficit

On "cabinet drawer handles" (4,400 SV) Castella and Manovella are both at #3. On "kitchen cupboard handles" (1,600 SV) Castella at #7 sits ahead of Lo&Co at #9 with no Manovella presence. On "kitchen cupboard door handles" (1,900 SV) Castella at #9 and Lo&Co at #10 sit directly against each other.

All three of those wins trace to /collections/cabinet-handles, which confirms that page is already Google's preferred cabinet-cluster ranker. It just needs to beat the split with /collections/minimalist-cabinet-handles on the head terms.

02

Lo&Co's single-page strategy beats Castella's split-page problem on the two biggest head terms

Lo&Co's one /collections/handles page ranks on cabinet handles (#4), modern cabinet handles (#2), cupboard handles (#8), draw handle (#4), kitchen cabinet handles (#4), cabinet pulls (#5) and handles for cabinets (#10).

Castella's equivalent authority is split across two URLs, and on the head terms "cabinet handles" (4,400 SV) and "kitchen handles" (2,900 SV) Google picks the narrower minimalist subset page as the Castella result.

03

The door category is the real structural gap

13 of Castella's 16 absences from the entire 1 to 30 window are door or door-adjacent: door handle, door handles, door hinges, front door levers, interior door handles, interior door lever, lockable door handles, sliding door handle, sliding door handles and the magnetic door stop variants. Combined monthly search volume on the clean door set is roughly 50,000.

Manovella owns the door handle cluster (door handle #5, door handles #8, interior door lever #5, door hinges #3) and Lo&Co has a top-10 foothold via /collections/entry. Castella is absent from the entire window on every door query. This is the single largest structural gap on the site.

04

Castella still owns the finish-led queries

Castella is #1, #2 and #3 on gold, brass and black cabinet handles respectively. Neither Manovella nor Lo&Co is meaningfully targeting finish-led style terms. This is a genuine defensive moat and worth protecting deliberately rather than by accident.

05

Lo&Co owns the knobs category

Lo&Co is #1 for "cabinet knobs" (3,600 SV) and #2 for "kitchen knobs" (260 SV) via a dedicated /collections/knobs page. Neither Castella nor Manovella has a comparable knobs collection in the top 10. Castella has a page-2 signal at #12 via the narrower /collections/round-cabinet-knobs.

The page-targeting problem

Highest value fix

Castella has two near-duplicate cabinet handle collection pages, and Google cannot tell which one is canonical.

The conflict

/collections/cabinet-handles is intended as the hero category page. /collections/minimalist-cabinet-handles is intended as a style-filtered subset. In live SERPs, Google picks the hero page on every long-tail variant but switches to the minimalist subset on the three largest head terms.

The most likely reason: the hero page's title, H1 and product mix skew sculptural and statement rather than minimalist, so on short-tail terms the minimalist page reads as the cleaner topical match.

The Lo&Co contrast

Lo&Co runs one /collections/handles page and lets it absorb the entire cluster. Modifier-led queries such as "modern cabinet handles", "cupboard handles" and "drawer handles" still land there. Lo&Co does not split by style for SEO purposes, accepting instead that a single page with strong topical breadth will outrank several narrower pages. Their live positions are the proof.

Why this matters commercially

A user searching "cabinet handles" today sees Castella at #5 on a style-narrowed subset page, sandwiched by Manovella at #2 and Lo&Co at #4, both ranking on broader collection pages. Beyond the ranking cost, the visitor asked to see the full range and lands on a filtered slice of it, so conversion suffers as well. A broader Castella hero page would likely move Castella from #5 into the top 3 and lift "kitchen handles" (2,900 SV) and "cabinet pulls" (2,900 SV) in the same motion.

Recommended fix sequence

  1. Decide which URL is the hero for "cabinet handles" and "kitchen handles". Recommendation: /collections/cabinet-handles. Cleaner URL, closer to the primary query, and already winning every long-tail variant in the cluster.
  2. Rework the hero page content so the on-page copy, H1, meta title and product selection all point at the category head term. Lead with the full range and let style (minimalist, sculptural, contemporary, modern) sit as a filter inside the page rather than as separate competing collections.
  3. Repurpose /collections/minimalist-cabinet-handles as a legitimate style subpage with a differentiated H1, intro copy about the minimalist style specifically, and internal links pointing up to the hero page as parent. If product overlap is above roughly 80%, consider a 301 to a filtered view of the hero page and retire the standalone URL.
  4. Audit internal linking. Every header nav, footer and inline link that says "Cabinet Handles" should point to the hero URL only. Today's mixed internal links signal to Google that the two pages are interchangeable.
  5. Confirm canonical tags. The minimalist page should either canonical to itself, if it is kept as a genuinely distinct subpage, or to the hero page if it is essentially duplicate. Current behaviour suggests canonicals are not doing the work they should.
Expected value

This is the highest expected-value fix on the site. It affects roughly 10,200 SV of head-term queries directly (cabinet handles, kitchen handles, cabinet pulls, modern cabinet handles) and consolidates the cluster around a page already ranking for six other variants. Monitor the four head terms weekly for about six weeks after the change ships.

4. Technical and on-page hygiene on priority pages

Foundation

A targeted review of the 10 pages that matter most: cabinet-handles, minimalist-cabinet-handles, door-hardware, the three finish pages and the homepage.

  • Title tags: primary keyword plus brand plus one supporting term
  • H1 present, singular and keyword-led
  • Meta description under 160 characters, written for click-through rather than ranking
  • Core Web Vitals passing
  • Product count visible, since users and crawlers both value category depth
  • Internal link count into each hero page, targeting 10 or more contextual links

Strategic investments

3 to 12 months

Each of these depends on a product range decision first. SEO without product depth converts poorly and dilutes category authority, so the range question is the gate on all of them.

Build out the door hardware category

~50,000 SV

The door category represents roughly 50,000 SV where Castella is invisible and at least one competitor is visible. 13 of Castella's 16 absences fall here. It is the single largest structural gap on the site.

Castella today has one broad /collections/door-hardware page ranking #9 for "door pulls", and no standalone pages for interior door handles, privacy or lockable handles, hinges, entry levers, sliding door handles or magnetic door stops. Manovella runs a dedicated page per type and ranks accordingly.

  1. Confirm the range question first. Does Castella stock enough door hardware SKUs to sustain 3 to 4 dedicated collection pages? If the range is thin, one strong /collections/door-hardware page with rich internal structure is the better bet than several thin pages.
  2. If the range supports it, build: an interior or internal door handles collection targeting "door handle" and "door handles"; an entry or front door handles collection targeting "front door levers"; a privacy or lockable door handles collection; and a door hinges collection.
  3. Content and linking. Each page needs 200 to 400 words of category copy, internal links from the existing door hardware hub, and at least one supporting blog post pointing in.
Expected outcome at 12 monthsTop-10 presence on 3 to 4 of the major door queries. Capturing even half of the combined Manovella and Lo&Co visibility here would be a significant lift on a category Castella currently cedes entirely.

Build a knobs collection

3,600 SV, Lo&Co owned

Lo&Co holds #1 for "cabinet knobs" (3,600 SV) and #2 for "kitchen knobs" (260 SV) via a dedicated /collections/knobs page. Manovella is absent. Castella sits at #12 via the narrower /collections/round-cabinet-knobs.

  1. Confirm the knob range (cabinet, kitchen, drawer, furniture) is deep enough to sustain a collection.
  2. Either broaden /collections/round-cabinet-knobs into a general knobs collection, or build a new /collections/cabinet-knobs page targeting "cabinet knobs" as the primary query.
  3. Lead the H1 and meta title with "cabinet knobs" rather than "round cabinet knobs" or "handles".
  4. Add internal links from the cabinet-handles hero, the homepage and the finish pages.

The existing page-2 signal shows Google already maps the site to the query, so this is a challenge to an incumbent rather than a cold start.

Close the drawer pull and drawer handle vocabulary gap

~23,400 SV

Castella sits on page 2 via /collections/cabinet-handles across most of this cluster: cabinet drawer pulls #13, drawer pull handle #12, draw handle #17, drawer pull #12, cupboard handles #26, and absent on drawer handle and drawer handles. Combined volume at stake is about 23,400 monthly searches.

Option A, lighter and recommended: rebuild /collections/cabinet-handles as a broader vocabulary hub, explicitly including "drawer pulls", "drawer handles", "draw handle" and "cupboard handles" in H2s, intro copy, product descriptions and internal anchors.

Option B, heavier: create a dedicated drawer pulls or drawer handles collection surfacing the product subset best described by those terms, internally linked from the cabinet handles hero.

Option A is the better initial bet. Google already maps the hero page to five of these queries on page 2, so the cheapest path to top 10 is making the existing ranking page more authoritative rather than splitting the signal across two pages.

Assess adjacent product categories before targeting

Range decision first

Before writing content for "magnetic door stop" (8,700 combined SV across variants), "sliding door handles" (4,800 combined) or "architectural hardware" (320), confirm Castella either carries the range or intends to.

If the range is there: dedicated collection pages one per category, clear internal link paths from the nearest parent hub, and at least one supporting content piece such as a buyer guide, finish guide or style pairing. If the range is not there, park these until the range decision is made. "Magnetic door stop" at 2,900 SV with no top-30 competitor is the single cleanest open-territory play in the priority set.

Content cluster to support the category pages

2 per month, 6 months

A small set of buyer-intent guides, each linking into the hero category page it supports. Keep them short, useful and imagery-led, at 800 to 1,200 words each.

  • "Brass vs gold vs black: choosing the right finish" into the finish pages
  • "Interior door handle styles explained" into the interior door handles page, if built
  • "What to look for in lockable door handles" into the privacy handles page
  • "Drawer pulls vs drawer handles: what's the difference" into the consolidated hero or drawer pulls page
  • "Cabinet knobs vs cabinet handles: when to choose which" into the cabinet knobs page, if built
