02 / 07Where the growth comes from

Google captures demand.
Meta creates it.

Google Ads · we manage this

Catches people already searching: the brand name, "Aboriginal art prints", "silk scarf Australia". Shopping and search campaigns, tuned properly, protect and convert that existing demand.

Worth doing. Expect incremental gains - the search volume is the ceiling.

Meta · where the growth is

Facebook and Instagram put the work in front of people who weren't looking for it. For a visual, emotional, story-rich brand, that's where new customers come from - and the creative that earns attention organically is the same creative that scales as paid.

This is the growth channel. The rest of this deck is how we win it.

Both issues below are Meta problems - and both are creative problems, not budget problems.

03 / 07Issue 1 · The audience mismatch

The imagery is talking to an 18-year-old.
The core buyer is 45+.

The scarves, wraps and kaftans are photographed on models around 18 years old. The person who actually buys a $129 silk artwork scarf is a 45+ woman - buying for herself, for her home, for what the piece means.

Core audience

Women 45+. Values-driven, story-motivated, happy to pay for meaning and provenance. Every hero image, model choice and ad set should be built for her - a woman she recognises, wearing the piece the way she would wear it.

Secondary audience

Men buying a gift for their wife. The story card in every box is the gift-giver's dream: he isn't just buying a scarf, he's giving her a story she can retell. Gift-framed creative and targeting, peaking at Christmas, Mother's Day and birthdays.

Worth a mention

Same pattern on the silk ties: shown on young men, while the likely buyer is a 50+ man (or his partner). A small fix, same principle.

Right story, wrong face on it. Recast the creative to match the buyer, and the same products start converting.

04 / 07Issue 2 · The story, executed properly

You already started
"The Story Behind the Work."
Now we build the machine.

The instinct was right - the execution is where it wins or loses. We've catalogued every story on both websites into a content database: 722 unique stories across 849 products, each with a scripted hook and spoken script ready for filming.

The series: one fixed setting - Saretta beside the easel, or beside the mannequin wearing the scarf - and one variable: the story. Same spot, same framing, every episode. She tells the story of one work in 30-40 seconds. That repetition is deliberate: it's how the audience learns the show, and how the algorithm learns the audience.

Hook - the spoken opening line, scripted per story. "Ever wondered why the wombat has a flat forehead? He earned it."

Story - Saretta tells it, word-for-word from her own published stories. Nothing invented, nothing paraphrased. Her voice, always.

Reveal - the full work, now that you can read it. The same design looks different once you know what it says.

Question - one line the audience can answer in the comments. The comments pick the next episodes.

Why one setting sells everything: the story is the product. Tell the Corroboree story once, from the same chair, and it sells at every price point:

05 / 07The show, kept simple

One set. One story at a time.
One hundred days.

The series runs as "The Story Behind the Work - 100 Days, 100 Stories." Same set, same framing, same structure every single episode. Only two things ever change: the painting, and its story.

Fixed · never changes

A plain wall. An easel, or one painting hung on it. Same corner, same framing, same light. Saretta presenting, the same episode structure (hook, story, reveal, question), and the same title card: the Awabakal word, with a small "Day 14 / 100" counter.

This is how the artists in our research shoot - a clean wall and the work. Nothing competes with the painting.

Variable · changes daily

The painting on the easel, and the story Saretta tells about it. That's the whole production decision each day - which work, which story. Everything else is already decided.

One variable is what makes 100 days achievable - and what makes the show instantly recognisable.

Easy to record

One permanent set means batch filming: a single session films a week or two of episodes. No location decisions, no setup time, no reasons to skip a day.

Familiar to watch

The audience learns the show in one glance - same wall, new story - and the algorithm learns exactly who to show it to. Repetition is the strategy, not a limitation.

Built to finish

The day counter turns viewers into followers (they follow to see it finished) and makes the 100-day commitment public. The spoken opening is always the story hook - never "welcome to day 14".

100 days is season one. The stories won't run out - there are 722 in the bank.
