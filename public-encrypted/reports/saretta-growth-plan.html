03 / 05Issue 1 · The audience mismatch

The imagery is talking to an 18-year-old.
The buyer is 45+.

The scarves, wraps and kaftans are photographed on models around 18 years old. The person who actually buys a $129 silk artwork scarf is a 45+ woman - buying for herself, for her home, for what the piece means.

Core audience

Women 45+. Values-driven, story-motivated, happy to pay for meaning and provenance. Every hero image, model choice and ad set should be built for her - a woman she recognises, wearing the piece the way she would wear it.

Secondary audience

Men buying a gift for their wife. The story card in every box is the gift-giver's dream: he isn't just buying a scarf, he's giving her a story she can retell. Gift-framed creative and targeting, peaking at Christmas, Mother's Day and birthdays.

Worth a mention

Same pattern on the silk ties: shown on young men, while the likely buyer is a 50+ man (or his partner). A small fix, same principle.

Right story, wrong face on it. Recast the creative to match the buyer, and the same products start converting.

04 / 05Issue 2 · The story, executed properly

You already started
"The Story Behind the Work."
Now we build the machine.

The instinct was right - the execution is where it wins or loses. We've catalogued every story on both websites into a content database: 722 unique stories across 849 products, each with a scripted hook and spoken script ready for filming.

The series: one fixed setting - Saretta beside the easel, or beside the mannequin wearing the scarf - and one variable: the story. Same spot, same framing, every episode. She tells the story of one work in 30-40 seconds. That repetition is deliberate: it's how the audience learns the show, and how the algorithm learns the audience.

Hook - the spoken opening line, scripted per story. "Ever wondered why the wombat has a flat forehead? He earned it."

Story - Saretta tells it, word-for-word from her own published stories. Nothing invented, nothing paraphrased. Her voice, always.

Reveal - the full work, now that you can read it. The same design looks different once you know what it says.

Question - one line the audience can answer in the comments. The comments pick the next episodes.

Why one setting sells everything: the story is the product. Tell the Corroboree story once, from the same chair, and it sells at every price point:
