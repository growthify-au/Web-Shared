You've probably heard Rob tell a story before.

For more than 40 years Rob Brough's job was finding out what happened and telling Queensland about it, on radio, on national television and on the evening news. Now he uses those same instincts to tell the stories of Sunshine Coast businesses.

Four decades of telling Queensland's stories

1970s

Radio

Starts as an announcer at 4VL in Charleville, then 4WK Warwick, 4BC Brisbane and 4GG on the Gold Coast.

1980s

Television sport

Moves into television covering sport for the ABC in Brisbane, then presents sport on National Nine News until 1989.

1990 to 1995

Family Feud

Hosts the Australian version of Family Feud on Channel 7, seen nationally.

Until 2024

7NEWS regional Queensland

Almost 20 years presenting the evening news from the Sunshine Coast studio, up to seven local editions every weeknight, from Mackay to the Darling Downs.

2022

Dare to Care

Directs a 50-minute documentary on the history of the Island Charity Swim. It premieres at Kawana Event Cinemas and helps bring the event back.

[Year]

Old Mates: Gone But Never Forgotten

A film about a group of Coolum surf lifesavers and a lifetime of mateship, premiered at the Coolum Civic Centre.

Now

Business documentaries

Telling the stories of the businesses that built the Sunshine Coast, a handful of films a year.

“A newsreader gets a few seconds to make you care. A documentary gets a few minutes. Either way, you have to find the real story first.”

A journalist's ear

Decades of interviews taught Rob which question gets the honest answer, and when to stay quiet and let it come.

A local's address book

Rob has lived and worked on the Coast for decades, coaching rugby league at Kawana and telling its news every night.

A small number of films

Rob takes on only a handful of films a year, and he's there for every one: the interviews, the filming and the edit.
